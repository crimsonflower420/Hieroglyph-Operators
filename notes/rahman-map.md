# Rahman (2020/2023): chapter and claim map, read through the operator lens

**Book.** Md. Ridwanur Rahman, *Ancient Egyptian Hieroglyph as the Pictorial Scrutiny of Modern Linguistics: The First Research Work on Egyptian Hieroglyphic Language in the Bosom of Bangladesh*. First published 2020; ILIM Press edition 2023, Dhaka. ISBN 978-984-35-4321-9. It began as the author's 2014 thesis in English (Linguistics) at North South University (front matter). 130 PDF pages. Printed page = PDF page − 14.

**How to read this map.** Page numbers are the printed ones. Claims are paraphrased, not quoted. The book's figures are not reproduced: the site redraws signs with Noto Sans Egyptian Hieroglyphs from our own bracket data. Status tags:
- **ATTESTED-AGREE**: matches the standard references (Gardiner 1957; Allen 2010; TLA where checked).
- **DIFFERS**: differs from the standard references; stated neutrally on the site, with a citation.
- **SPECULATIVE / NON-MAINSTREAM (推測/非主流)**: outside this site's scope; flagged, no derivation.
- **DERIVED dNN**: a two-layer derivation in `data/derivations/rahman.drv` (format SUBLEXICAL-DERIVATIONS-v1).

The author describes the book's mission (pp. 4–8, front matter, ch. I) as applying modern linguistics (phonetics, grammar, semantics, morphology, syntax, psycho- and sociolinguistics) to hieroglyphs. He says several pieces are his own contributions: the internal/external "evolutionary links", his choice of IPA symbols, and his treatment of clipping and compounding.

---

## Ch. I–II. Introduction; Research method (pp. 1–7)
- The study is qualitative and library-based. Sources: secondary books and websites (Kinnaer, Millmore, Loy, Budge, Bruaw, Alsaadawi, among others). No primary corpus or museum object is examined.
- *Lens note:* almost every sign example comes from secondary web or popular sources. Where we derive one, we check it against Gardiner's sign list and the grammars.

## Ch. III. Origin of decipherment (pp. 8–13)
- Medieval Muslim scholars' contributions: "Ibn Wahishiya" (10th c.) and Abul-Qasim al-Iraqi (14th c.), via El Daly 2005 (pp. 8–9). Then the Rosetta Stone and Champollion 1822.
- Ptolemy/Cleopatra cartouches appear in the text here and again on pp. 53 and 84. → **d20**, **d26**.

## Ch. IV. "Language evolution through hieroglyphs" (pp. 14–24)
- **Internal evolutionary links** (pp. 14–17): hieroglyphic → hieratic → demotic → Coptic. The sequence is mainstream (**ATTESTED-AGREE** in outline). The book dates hieratic to "around 1600 BC" (p. 15). Standard histories put hieratic alongside hieroglyphs from the early dynastic period (**DIFFERS**, minor). The site mentions this only in the map; no derivation (single-sign script history, not combination).
- **External evolutionary links** (pp. 18–24): Proto-Sinaitic from Egyptian signs is in outline a mainstream hypothesis (Goldwasser is cited). The book then goes further, following Alsaadawi:
  - Latin letters a, b, K, M, R, T, W, X, Z "taken from" specific hieroglyphs by shape and sound (pp. 20–21);
  - Square Hebrew letters "copied directly" from hieroglyphs (pp. 21–22);
  - Arabic letters derived from hieroglyphs, "directly" or "indirectly visible" (pp. 22–24);
  - "Semitic" from a hieroglyphic /sm/ (p. 18).
  → **SPECULATIVE / NON-MAINSTREAM**. Out of scope, no derivation.

## Ch. V. Phonetic values of signs (pp. 25–38)
- **Sign classes** (pp. 25–34): ideograms (= determinatives, in the book's usage) vs phonograms; phonograms are uni-, bi- and triliteral ("unilateral/bilateral/trilateral").
  - dpt "boat" + boat determinative to avoid confusion with a name (p. 26, Millmore). → **d09** (ATTESTED-AGREE).
  - ḥr: face sign as phonogram in ḥryt "dread" vs ideogram ḥr "face" (p. 33). → **d08** (agree; the book does not discuss the Z1 stroke).
  - Ramesses: rejects an alphabetic modern spelling, gives sun–F31–S29–S29 (pp. 33–34). → **d21** (agree).
  - Sneferu cartouche S29–F35–D21–G43; only F35 triliteral (p. 34). → **d07** (identification agrees; r read twice DIFFERS).
  - Hieroglyphic "Mary" and "Messiah" (p. 34, Alsaadawi). → **SPECULATIVE / NON-MAINSTREAM**.
- **Three-vowel IPA scheme** (pp. 27–28; App. 2 pp. 105–108): vulture = /ɑ:/, forearm = /ɑ/, reed = /I/, plus an articulatory classification of consonants by English IPA categories (pp. 28–34), e.g. loaf X1 as a dental. → **SPECULATIVE / NON-MAINSTREAM** as a phonological claim (Egyptological convention treats ꜣ, ꜥ, j as consonants). Out of scope, no derivation.
- **Aspiration** (pp. 35–36): šmw/"ʃkht", Khufu "khwfw". → out of scope (phonetics, not combination).
- **Elision & assimilation** (pp. 36–38): the "authentic" vs "bookish" pronunciations. *Lens:* every example is a phonetic-complement spelling that the book reads with the complement sounded:
  - pꜣ G40+G1 → **d01**; pꜣ (verb) Q3+G40+G1 → **d02**; ḥb V28+D58+W3 → **d03** (two candidate B trees); Šw H6+G43+A40 → **d04**; mri U7+D21+A2 → **d05**; sḏm F21+G17 → **d06**.
  - Not derived: dbḥ "beg", sjqr "enrich", smn "make firm", sꜣḫ "glorify". In each, one sign in the book's small figure could not be identified with confidence, and partial data are not entered.
  - Standard view: complements repeat consonants and are not pronounced again (**DIFFERS**).

## Ch. VI. Grammar (pp. 39–48)
- Parts of speech, with examples largely from Loy, Kinnaer, Budge, Knight and Millmore (pp. 39–45).
- **Genitive and honorific order** (p. 40): direct genitive is possessed + possessor; a god or king as possessor is written first but comes after grammatically (Kinnaer). Example ḥwt-nṯr. → **d19** (rule agrees; the book's transcription keeps written order).
- **Number** (pp. 40–41): plural strokes; sn/snw, snt, nṯr/nṯrw, dual -wj/-j, numerals after nouns. → **d14**, **d15**, **d17**.
- **Gender** (p. 41): feminine -t before female determinative; z/zt, sn/snt, ḥm/ḥmt. → **d16**.
- Pronouns, demonstratives (pp. 41–42); adjectives nfrt + different determinatives (pp. 42–43). → **d10**.
- Verb-determinatives and "perfect verbs" as phonogram + ideogram (p. 43); adverbs with r; prepositions; conjunctions; interjection "hj" (pp. 43–46).
- Articles pꜣ/tꜣ/nꜣ; particles; tense: sḏm.f / sḏm.n.f, iw.f r smr (pp. 46–48). → **d18** (gloss "to think" DIFFERS; n as past agrees).

## Ch. VII. Semantics (pp. 49–50)
- Antonyms, synonyms, homonyms with sign examples. No sign-combination claim beyond spellings. Not derived.

## Ch. VIII. Morphology and syntax (pp. 51–58)
- **Bruaw's three uses of a sign** (sound / thing / idea), worked on a "door" example (pp. 51–52). Not derived (the figure is a reproduced chart).
- **Clipping** (p. 53), which the author says is his own contribution:
  - nswt → sw (M23 alone) → **d23**: the standard reading is an abbreviated *writing* of the same word (**DIFFERS** in kind: graphic, not morphological).
  - mꜣꜥ-ḫrw → mꜣꜥ; kꜣ-nḫt → kꜣ: not derived (figures too small to identify the reduced forms with confidence).
  - "Cleopatratsa": the cartouche read with the final X1+H8 sounded; "Cleopatra" called a clipping → **d26** (**DIFFERS**: H8 is a silent determinative; Champollion 1822, Callimahos).
- **Compounding** (pp. 53–56), also the author's own: "phonogram + ideogram" compounds, called endocentric because the rightmost ideogram gives the class.
  - iꜣwt + old man → **d12**; mwt + woman → **d11**; mri + man → **d05**; ḥsi + man → **d13**.
  - Not derived: psḏ, nis, ḥꜥpy, srḫ, swri, sbṯ, z (figure identification).
  - *Lens finding:* what the book calls compounding is what standard grammars call a determinative closing a word. The book's "rightmost member gives the class" corresponds closely to v1's head marking on the determinative (DET_H). Same observation, different label.
- **Syntax** (pp. 56–58): SVO "off and on" (Budge example), VSO as standard (Loy), iw-sentences (Knight). *Out of scope* for derivations: this site makes no syntax claims and draws no sentence trees.

## Ch. IX. Reading and translation (pp. 59–68)
- **Reading direction** (pp. 59–60, Kinnaer): figures face the beginning of the line; rows and columns; symmetric door-jamb texts.
- **Reading order within quadrats** (pp. 60–61, Bruaw): read in the line direction, then downward; signs above come first. Numbered example 1–7. → **d25**. The rule is the definition of WALK_write over layer A; agrees.
- **Word-for-word vs sense-for-sense translation** (p. 61). Demonstrated on the **Kariong "hieroglyphs"** (pp. 61–66, Senff), which the book treats as proof of Egyptians in Australia. → **SPECULATIVE / NON-MAINSTREAM**; no derivation.
- Rosetta formula "Ptolemy, ever-living, beloved of Ptah" (pp. 65–66; also p. 84). → **d20** (honorific transposition of Ptḥ before mry).
- **Perceptions of decipherers** (pp. 66–68): Alsaadawi's readings S34+S29 as "salam", Amun as "Iman", the double crown as "nabi". → **SPECULATIVE / NON-MAINSTREAM**.

## Ch. X. Psycholinguistics (pp. 69–80)
- Left/right-brain account of "decipherment" (Sperry); conceptualization (lion signs, Sphinx), signification (Saussure; the star sign; the Alexander cartouche), verbalization (Levelt's lemma/lexeme). Chomsky is mentioned.
- *Out of scope.* The site claims nothing about psychological derivation (v1 §7). No derivation.

## Ch. XI. Sociolinguistics (pp. 80–89)
- Royal titles: the crowns; nswt-bjtj read /swtnt-nt/ (p. 81) → **d22** (reading DIFFERS; 2×2 quadrat crosses the function grouping). nswt, ḥm, ity, nb (p. 84); pr-ꜥꜣ "Great House" (p. 84) → **d24** (agree).
- Hyksos ḥqꜣw-ḫꜣswt (p. 83); Pharaoh vs Malik discussion via Harun Yahya (pp. 82–84).
- "Israel in Egypt" (pp. 85–88): Isr, Goshen, Pithom pr-itm. Mixes mainstream items (Pithom = pr-itm) with Alsaadawi readings → **SPECULATIVE / NON-MAINSTREAM** where it follows Alsaadawi.
- Mummification (pp. 88–89): pyramid signs read /mr/; mummy determinatives.

## Ch. XII. Conclusion and suggestion (pp. 90–91)
- Restates linguistics of hieroglyphs as form / meaning / context; proposes Egyptology departments in Bangladesh.

## Appendices
- **App. 1 (pp. 98–104), Dendera zodiac**: alphabet from astronomy (Moran, Pellar); zodiac figures read as hieroglyphic words. → **SPECULATIVE / NON-MAINSTREAM**.

## Open questions
1. d03 ḥb: complements + W3 as PHON2, or a spelling closed by W3 as determinative? Both pass the checker.
2. d22 nswt-bjtj (and nswt generally): honorific or graphic transposition? Allen suggests transposition; Poe calls it graphic.
3. d20: orientation of the book's Ptolemy figure (sequence R→L, animal signs facing left).
4. Should v1 add a reorder class for quadrat-internal "column" reading (d22), distinct from honorific transposition?
5. Signs not identified in the book's figures (dbḥ, sjqr, smn, sꜣḫ, psḏ, nis, mꜣꜥ-ḫrw short forms). These could be completed from Gardiner or TLA spellings, but those would then be standard spellings, not the book's.
